**Status: non-normative practice material.** Not part of the Transformation Decision Record standard.

## What it is

Ten questions asked of **one policy**, to establish whether that policy is governed as a document or as a connected collection of decisions.

It is a diagnostic a reader can run on their own estate. It produces a finding about traceability. **It is not an assessment, a maturity score or a benchmark**, and no result from it should be compared across organisations.

## The condition

Answer all ten **without** rereading the full policy estate, convening the original authors, or reconstructing meeting history.

That constraint is the whole test. Almost any organisation can answer these questions given enough archaeology; the question is whether the answers are retrievable.

## The ten questions

1. **Which consequential decisions does this policy currently contain?**
2. **Which statements are external obligations you inherited, and which are internal interpretations, organisational choices, delegated decisions, controls or evidence requirements?**
3. **Who held the authority for each, and under what mandate?**
4. **What was chosen, against which alternatives and on what evidence?**
5. **What binding effect, boundary and validity does each carry?**
6. **Which systems, processes, contracts, controls and mandates implement each one?**
7. **Which evidence shows each is operating as intended?**
8. **Which later decision superseded or narrowed any of them?**
9. **What else must be reconsidered if one of them changes?**
10. **Would an authorised person and an authorised machine retrieve the same current answer, for the same authorised view?**

## Reading the result

> If those answers require document archaeology, the policy is governed as a document rather than as a connected decision collection.

That is the finding. It is structural, not a judgement about the people who wrote the policy — a document designed to communicate a position is not designed to keep each decision inside it independently retrievable. Most were never asked to.

**Question 10 is the one most often misread.** It is not about automation. It asks whether the answer is *stable and retrievable*, and it says *for the same authorised view* deliberately: two authorised people may legitimately receive different views of the same estate, and that is the discipline working, not a failure.

## Two boundaries

**External rules are obligation collections. Internal policies are decision collections.** A regulator sets the obligation; the firm decides how to interpret and meet it. Only the second is the firm's own judgement, and separating them is what makes the firm's position retrievable at all.

**A principle is often a decision that has not finished deciding** — it becomes operationally useful when someone supplies authority, effect, boundary and validity. Some principles are deliberately enduring constraints and should stay that way. Do not force every principle into a decision record.

## Scope

Run it on **one policy** that a live change touches. Running it across a policy estate turns a diagnostic into a programme, which is not what this is for.
